# Adverbial clauses

Four original English examples cover concession, purpose, degree and result,
and an absolute participial construction. Each record includes its construction,
sentence, and explanation. The absolute clause has its own subject; the result
construction uses a degree modifier with a following result clause. These are
related clause constructions, not a claim that all four have identical syntax.

All four records were individually reviewed for grammar, explanation, meaningful
examples, self-contained output, and honest provenance. The purpose example now
says “its contents” instead of “it”: the original wording suggested mistaking a
jar itself for sugar. IDs and the other records were preserved. Validation checked
the three required string fields, nonblank values, no NUL characters, unique IDs,
and unique texts (4 records, 4 IDs, 4 texts).

The examples are editorial inventions, not an imported dataset or quotations.
The purpose/result distinction and degree-result form were checked against
Cambridge's [purpose explanation](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/so-that-or-in-order-that)
and [so grammar](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/so).
