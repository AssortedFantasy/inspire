# Market clearing

Continuous quantities and a competitive partial-equilibrium market with linear
inverse demand `P_b = A - bQ` and marginal cost `P_s = c + sQ`, both slopes
strictly positive. Prices, quantities, transfers and welfare are exact reduced
fractions. Utility and cost are quasilinear. There are no externalities.

Six equally sampled regimes cover ordinary clearing, unequal/equal tax incidence,
ceilings, floors, taxes at or around market shutdown, and a hard production cap.
Controls include binding, nonbinding and equality cases. Binding controls trade
the entire short side without government purchases. Rationing among buyers or
sellers is unspecified, so the generator does not assign welfare to controls.

A capped supply curve becomes vertical at its capacity. Tax samples straddle
the point where scarcity rent disappears and capacity becomes slack. Printed
capacity rent is the marginal price-minus-cost gap, not total producer surplus.
Tax welfare loss is the reduction in the demand-minus-cost integral, including
tax revenue as a transfer. At zero trade, buyer and seller transaction prices
are not reported because no unique traded price exists.

Tests check market clearing and the tax wedge, capacity complementarity, exact
tax and surplus accounting, shutdown and capacity thresholds, and the independent
quantity equations on both sides of a control boundary.

Algorithm conventions follow [MIT 14.01SC, Applying Supply and Demand](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-01sc-principles-of-microeconomics-fall-2011/pages/unit-1-supply-and-demand/applying-supply-and-demand/)
and [Principles of Welfare Economics](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-01sc-principles-of-microeconomics-fall-2011/pages/unit-4-welfare-economics/principles-of-welfare-economics/).
All instances and implementation are original. No source text is embedded.
